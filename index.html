01 • Problem Statement

The Issue: What is the specific challenge?

Indigenous highland vegetable farmers—predominantly Ibaloi and Kankanaey communities in Benguet and Mountain Province—form the backbone of Philippine temperate vegetable production. Benguet alone supplies roughly 89% of the country’s potatoes, 82% of carrots, 81% of Baguio pechay, 67% of cabbage, and 56% of chayote.

Despite anchoring national vegetable security, these smallholder farmers experience severe income instability, recurring household debt cycles, post-harvest spoilage exceeding 40% in conventional supply chains, and sudden farmgate price crashes. Whenever farmgate prices drop below production and hauling costs, growers absorb net financial losses and are frequently forced to leave mature crops unharvested or discard unsold rotting produce.

Core Paradox: While lowland retail vegetable prices remain elevated for consumers, highland IP farmers capture only a thin fraction of the final retail peso while absorbing nearly all weather, spoilage, and price volatility risks.

02 • Structural Drivers

Root Cause: Why does this problem exist?

Supply Chain Architecture Comparison

Switch between the prevailing spot-market channel at the La Trinidad Vegetable Trading Post and a shortened institutional model.

01

Multi-Layered, Intermediary-Heavy Supply Chains

Over 90% of major crops—such as cabbage (96%) and broccoli (91%)—pass through long, circuitous spot-market channels (farmer → disposer → buyer → wholesaler-retailer → retailer) centered at hubs like the La Trinidad Vegetable Trading Post. Multiple middlemen capture the largest share of retail markups, while farmers lack real-time price visibility.

02

Informal Credit Dependency (“Supply-Financing” Trap)

Lacking formal land collateral or accessible bank credit, smallholder growers rely on private financiers and traders (disposers) for seeds, fertilizers, and farm inputs. In return, farmers are contractually or informally bound to sell harvests exclusively to their financier at dictated prices, leaving minimal net earnings after debt deductions.

03

Inadequate Cold-Chain & Processing Infrastructure

Because highland vegetables are highly perishable and local agro-processing remains limited, the shortage of accessible cold storage facilities forces farmers to sell immediately upon harvest regardless of depressed prices, accelerating spoilage during transit delays or highway bottlenecks.

04

Climate Hazards & Rising Input Costs

Cordillera farms are regularly battered by typhoons, intense monsoon rains, and seasonal frost events that wipe out standing crops, while volatile fuel and synthetic fertilizer prices steadily inflate production and highland-to-lowland hauling costs.

05

Import Surges & Agricultural Smuggling

Unchecked importation and technical smuggling of low-priced foreign vegetables—most notably carrots and cabbage—flood lowland wholesale markets, artificially depressing demand and severely undercutting farmgate prices for local Cordillera IP growers.

03 • Actionable Interventions

Proposed Solution: What is a viable, fact-based way to address it?

Pillar 1 • Market Access

Shorten the Value Chain via Direct Institutional Linkages

Scale up structured direct-marketing agreements—such as the Department of Agriculture–Cordillera’s (DA-CAR) Kadiwa ng Pangulo and direct hauling programs—that connect organized IP farmer cooperatives and irrigators’ associations directly to institutional buyers (supermarkets, hotels, hospitals, and LGUs).

  • Eliminates redundant spot-market middlemen layers
  • Establishes transparent, pre-agreed floor pricing
  • Builds collective bargaining power for smallholder growers
Pillar 2 • Post-Harvest Infrastructure

Expand Cooperative-Managed Cold Storage & Processing

Accelerate the construction and cooperative operation of regional cold storage facilities—coordinated by DA-CAR and the Benguet Agri-Pinoy Trading Center (BAPTC)—paired with cost-efficient vegetable processing equipment.

  • Allows farmers to hold surplus harvests during market gluts
  • Reduces the >40% post-harvest spoilage loss rate
  • Converts excess or cosmetically imperfect produce into shelf-stable goods
Pillar 3 • Risk & Trade Protection

Strengthen Crop Insurance & Anti-Smuggling Enforcement

Expand farmer enrollment in the Registry System for Basic Sectors in Agriculture (RSBSA) to widen access to Philippine Crop Insurance Corporation (PCIC) indemnity payouts, alongside stricter Bureau of Customs (BOC) and DA warehouse inspections.

  • Provides rapid financial recovery after typhoon and frost damage
  • Breaks reliance on predatory informal financiers after crop failure
  • Halts technical smuggling of carrots, cabbage, and temperate crops
